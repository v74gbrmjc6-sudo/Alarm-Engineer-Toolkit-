🔧 Fault Finder

Battery Fault

Check:

  • Battery age and condition.
  • Battery voltage with mains present.
  • Battery voltage under load.
  • Charging voltage from the panel.
  • Battery terminals and connections.
A battery can show a reasonable voltage with no load but collapse when the system demands current.

Auxiliary / Power Fault

  • Check panel output voltage.
  • Check for short circuits on auxiliary supply.
  • Disconnect external loads one at a time.
  • Check PIRs, expanders and external devices.

Zone Fault

  • Identify the affected zone.
  • Check device physically.
  • Check cable continuity.
  • Check for shorts/opens.
  • Check EOL resistance.
  • Check tamper circuit.

Tamper Fault

  • Check device lid.
  • Check back tamper.
  • Check cable tamper.
  • Check panel terminals.
  • Check for loose connections.
  • Check whether the fault follows a particular device.

Bell Tamper

If a bell tamper appears immediately after a bell activation, check the bell supply voltage while the bell is operating.
  • Check bell battery.
  • Check bell hold-off voltage.
  • Check tamper loop.
  • Check cable.
  • Check for voltage drop when bell activates.

Communications Fault

  • Check network/mobile signal.
  • Check communicator power.
  • Check Ethernet/router connection where applicable.
  • Check account/ATS details.
  • Check reporting configuration.
  • Check event log.

False Alarm

  • Check event log.
  • Identify exact device.
  • Check environmental causes.
  • Check insects/dust/condensation.
  • Check mounting.
  • Check sensitivity/programming.
  • Check whether the device has a history of repeat activations.

🚨 Texecom Premier

Common Panels

  • Premier Elite 24
  • Premier Elite 48
  • Premier Elite 64-W
  • Premier Elite 88
  • Premier Elite 640

Useful Checks

  • Enter engineer mode before carrying out programming changes where required.
  • Check the event log before replacing equipment.
  • Check mains and battery voltages.
  • Check zone resistance and tamper.
  • Check keypad/expander addressing.
  • Check Ricochet device status.
On a fault that appears intermittently, the event log is often more useful than what the keypad is displaying right now.

Texecom Ricochet

  • Check device battery.
  • Check signal strength.
  • Check device supervision.
  • Check mesh/network status.
  • Check nearby powered Ricochet devices.
  • Consider whether the building has changed since installation.

SmartCom

  • Check SmartCom power.
  • Check network connection.
  • Check router/internet.
  • Check SmartCom configuration.
  • Check app/cloud connection.
  • Check panel communication settings.
  • Check event reporting.
Do not assume a SmartCom fault is caused by the SmartCom itself. Check panel configuration, network and power first.

🛠️ Installation Reference

Before Starting

  • Confirm panel type and software/firmware where relevant.
  • Identify all existing zones.
  • Check existing wiring before removing anything.
  • Photograph existing connections.
  • Check available power capacity.
  • Confirm customer requirements.

Zone Wiring

  • Check correct zone configuration.
  • Confirm EOL method.
  • Check resistor values against the panel specification.
  • Check tamper wiring.
  • Check cable joints.
  • Check polarity where applicable.

PIR Installation

  • Mount securely.
  • Avoid pointing directly at heat sources.
  • Avoid unstable mounting surfaces.
  • Consider sunlight and environmental conditions.
  • Check coverage.
  • Walk test after installation.

External Bell

  • Check cable route.
  • Check bell battery.
  • Check hold-off supply.
  • Check tamper loop.
  • Check cover/back tamper.
  • Test bell activation.
  • Check voltage during activation.

Keypads

  • Confirm address.
  • Confirm bus wiring.
  • Check voltage at the keypad.
  • Check for intermittent bus faults.

📡 Wireless

Wireless Device Not Responding

  1. Check battery.
  2. Check device tamper.
  3. Check signal strength.
  4. Check device programming.
  5. Check supervision settings.
  6. Check wireless expander/receiver.

Ricochet Tips

  • Powered devices can help form the mesh.
  • Do not assume the nearest device is necessarily the best route.
  • Large buildings and construction materials can affect radio performance.
  • Moving furniture or changing walls can affect wireless performance.
When troubleshooting wireless faults, check the device itself AND the wireless network around it.

📞 ARC / ATS / Communications

ATS Fault

  • Check communicator power.
  • Check network/mobile connection.
  • Check account details.
  • Check reporting path.
  • Check panel reporting configuration.
  • Check event log.

Dialling / Reporting Issues

  • Confirm telephone/IP configuration.
  • Check dialling attempts.
  • Check reporting areas.
  • Check receiver/account information.
  • Check whether the ARC is actually receiving signals.

SmartCom

  • Check Ethernet/network.
  • Check router.
  • Check communicator status.
  • Check cloud/app connection.
  • Check panel programming.
A communicator showing connected does not automatically prove that the ARC is receiving the required signals. Always verify reporting where possible.

⚡ Quick Fixes

Fault Appeared After Alarm Activation

Check what happened immediately before the fault. Look at the event sequence, not just the final fault displayed.

Intermittent Fault

  • Check event log.
  • Check loose connections.
  • Check cable joints.
  • Check voltage under load.
  • Check battery condition.
  • Check environmental causes.

Device Keeps Going Into Alarm

  • Check positioning.
  • Check environment.
  • Check device condition.
  • Check wiring.
  • Check programming.
  • Replace only after establishing the likely cause.

Bell Fault

Check the bell battery, hold-off voltage, tamper circuit and voltage during activation before replacing the bell.

The best engineers don't just replace the device showing the fault — they find out why the fault occurred.

💡 Engineer Tips

1. Check the Event Log

The event log can tell you the sequence of events and often reveals what actually caused an intermittent fault.

2. Measure Before Replacing

Voltage, resistance and continuity measurements can save unnecessary replacement of equipment.

3. Test Under Load

A supply may look healthy with no load but drop significantly when a device operates.

4. Don't Forget the Cable

A device fault isn't always a device fault. Check cable joints, terminals, crushed cable, corrosion and intermittent connections.

5. Take Photos

Photograph existing panel wiring and programming before making major changes.

6. Work Methodically

  1. Identify the fault.
  2. Understand the system.
  3. Check the event log.
  4. Measure.
  5. Isolate the problem.
  6. Repair.
  7. Test.
  8. Document.
A good fault-finding process is more valuable than memorising hundreds of fault codes.
